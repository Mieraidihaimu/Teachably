# Your personal starter pack

The pack starts with the seven interests you named. There are six activities per interest, giving 42 different family experiences before the matching deck repeats. The full library has 67 activities across 12 categories, with five activities in each of the other categories. Each activity has four flexible stages adding up to 30 minutes, a prompt for your own experience, and separate invitations for ages 2 and 6.

| Your interest | An example activity | An invitation at 2 | An invitation at 6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maths | Same number, new shape | Move large toys while you count together | Rearrange the same group and explain whether its total changed |
| Reading | Read a little, notice a lot | Point to a picture, make a sound, or turn a page with help | Make a prediction and find a clue in the book |
| Basic movement exercise | Move, freeze, notice | Choose or copy a comfortable small action | Plan a short sequence, pause, and describe how it feels |
| General physics | A little ramp lab | Release a large toy with help and watch it roll | Change a gentle slope while keeping the toy and starting point the same |
| Diary writing | A page of our day | Make marks or dictate a word for a shared page | Draw, dictate, or write a specific memory and a detail |
| Coding | Find the muddled instruction | Move a toy along a picture route with you | Find a mismatched instruction, change it, and test the sequence again |
| Critical thinking | A clue or a guess? | Point to something they notice on a familiar toy | Separate an observation from a guess and suggest a way to check it |

For a new profile using one suggestion a day, the daily picker covers all seven areas in each seven-day block. Choosing alternatives also counts those ideas as explored. In **My skills**, you can change the selection or use **Use my seven interests** to restore this toolkit. An existing saved profile keeps its choices until you change them.

Your two-year-old can join through play, gestures, sounds, or brief participation. Your six-year-old can try a question or small challenge. Drawing and dictation are available throughout the diary activities; writing words is optional. The half hour is shared family time, with breaks and an early finish whenever wanted.
